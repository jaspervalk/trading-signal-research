Designer pass — notes for review

Direction: "The Brief" — editorial, single-column reading flow. The page is a research note, not a dashboard.

Assumptions made:

  • Polarity is communicated via small typographic glyphs (▲ ▼ ◆) + sign in mono, never via colored fills — this preserves the calm palette and avoids reading as advice.
  • The chart is rendered as a static SVG silhouette; in production this would be lightweight-charts. A 365d daily-candle implementation would respect the same hairline grid + muted markers.
  • The "Historical only" badge slot is reserved in the header but not rendered (mock data is recent). The slot is sized so its later appearance doesn't reflow surrounding type.

Decisions & reasoning:

  • Light mode against a dark dashboard — the ticker page is the deliberate "reading room." Switching modes signals "you're here to slow down and read evidence," not to skim KPIs. The inverse-mode contrast also makes the page memorable as the research surface.
  • Claims as italic pull quotes, not table rows — the verbatim quote IS the artefact. Centering 22-23px Newsreader italic forces the eye to read it; metadata demotes to fineprint above and below.
  • Wilson CIs in prose, not parentheses-only — "Hit rate 42% (95% CI lower bound)" reads as a sentence; reviewers complained that bare "0.42" looked like a composite score.

Tradeoffs flagged:

  • Single-column reading flow means the signal strip can't be glanced at the same time as the chart — intentional but worth user-testing against scanners who expect a dashboard.
  • "Listen at 3:14 →" assumes click-through to the source. We need to confirm the audio/timestamp deeplink contract with the YouTube adapter (segment-level seek).
  • Polarity glyphs (▲ ▼ ◆) without color may underweight directional signal for fast scanning — open to introducing a single-color tint (one slate value) on direction-only glyphs in v2.

Reference: Stripe Press product pages (Working in Public, High Growth Handbook) and Reuters Graphics long-form — both use serif display, single-column reading, and editorial restraint to make data pieces feel authored rather than dashboard'd.
